# NASA's principles for ethical AI

At a minimum, NASA AI must be:

%ai-principle[ethic.fair] **Fair.** AI systems must include considerations regarding how to treat people, including refining solutions to mitigate discrimination and bias, preventing covert manipulation, and supporting diversity and inclusion.

%ai-principle[ethic.transparent] **Explainable and Transparent.** Solutions must clearly state if, when, and how an AI system is involved, and AI logic and decisions must be explainable. AI solutions must protect intellectual property and include risk management in their construction and use. AI systems must be documented.

%ai-principle[ethic.accountable] **Accountable.** Organizations and individuals must be accountable for the systems they create, and organizations must implement AI governance structures to provide oversight. AI developers should consider potential misuse or misinterpretation of AI-derived results (intentional or otherwise) and take steps to mitigate negative impact.

%ai-principle[ethic.secure-safe] **Secure and Safe.** AI systems must respect privacy and do no harm. Humans must monitor and guide machine learning processes. AI system risk tradeoffs must be considered when determining benefit of use.

%ai-principle[ethic.human-centric] **Human-Centric and Societally Beneficial.** AI systems must obey human legal systems and must provide benefits to society. At the current state of AI, humans must remain in charge, though future advancements may cause reconsideration of this requirement.

%ai-principle[ethic.robust] **Scientifically and Technically Robust.** AI systems must adhere to the scientific method NASA applies to all problems, be informed by scientific theory and data, robustly tested in implementation, well-documented, and peer reviewed in the scientific community.
